At 50 % the one-shot router is at parity with the V0-style hard-label classifier (0.040 vs

**A caveat on the coded rule, and why it does not matter.** `verdicts.py` compares each
baseline at its validation-selected threshold and, when no threshold attains the budget, at its
cheapest point. The ensemble-uncertainty trigger cannot spend less than 0.40 of uniform fine,
so at the 30 % point it competes at 44 % more compute than the router. H1-V1 says "at fixed
compute"; the post-hoc supplement `verdicts_supplement.md` (written after the verdict was read,
same statistics, unattainable baselines listed instead of compared) gives, at 30 %, the
uncertainty-**per-cost** trigger (0.324, 0.418) against the router (0.277, 0.823): ratio 1.97,
Δ +0.405 [−0.020, +1.219], Holm p 0.38. Not ≤ 0.8, so **H1-V1 is falsified under both
readings**. At 15 % the router beats that trigger (1.46 vs 1.58, CI excluding 0) — a point at
which the hypothesis cannot pass. Reserved set (100 episodes, evaluated once at the same
thresholds): 30 % → 0.365 vs 0.360 (ratio 1.01); 50 % → 0.017 vs hard-label 0.046 (ratio 0.36,
CI includes 0). Same picture.

What the numbers say: the *spread* of the learned VoC ensemble is a better refinement signal
than its *mean prediction* at every budget the spread-triggers can reach; the mean regresses
rare, large gains toward zero (labels are zero-inflated and heavy-tailed even after the
log-compression of correction 3), whereas members disagree exactly on those rows. The written
rules are useless on this system: the medium-vs-coarse discrepancy is large for every channel
(the reduced level is far from the HH level everywhere), so the discrepancy and sensitivity
monitors cannot spend less than 54–60 % and still leave > 5 tolerances of error. This is the
reverse of V0, where the written physics rule was the strongest baseline at high budgets and
ensemble disagreement contributed nothing.

## 3. H3-V1 (selectivity vs coverage) — **not passed** (not falsified)

Precision at the router's principal-point recall (0.755): learned 0.668 vs discrepancy 0.256
(2.6×) and sensitivity × discrepancy 0.310 (2.2×) — the ≥ 1.2× clause holds. The crossing
clause fails: the router is better than the written rules at 15 % (as predicted) **and** at
50 % (not predicted: share 0.389 at 42 % vs router 0.049 at 49 %; discrepancy/sensitivity
> 5.6 at ≥ 54 %). The predicted "written rule wins by coverage at high budgets" did not happen
here; the router dominates the written rules at every budget, which is not the falsification
condition either ("dominated at every budget" or "precision not higher"). The sequential
hybrid formally passes H3 (1.84× / 1.54×, crossing true), but only because its own plateau at
0.58 makes the share rule "not worse" at 50 % — a property of the candidate filter, not of
coverage; reported, not claimed.

False-safe rate at the principal point (`verdicts.md`, table): one-shot VoC 0.17–0.24 across ID,
reserved and the four OOD families; hard-label 0.12–0.19; the uncertainty trigger 0.00 (it
refines nearly everything it is unsure about, at 40 % cost).
