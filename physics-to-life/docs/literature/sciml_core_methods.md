# Scientific Machine Learning — Core Methods: Literature Scan

**Scan date:** 2026-09-14
**Cluster:** SciML core methods (neural operators; ML closures; hybrid/grey-box models; differentiable simulators; UQ; simulation-based inference; active learning / BOED; equation discovery; causal representation learning; world models / MBRL; inverse design in biology; coarse-to-fine reconstruction and latent dynamics).

**Scope.** This scan covers the method families that Physics-to-Life must either adopt, adapt, or explicitly reject for its hierarchical world model that queries mechanistic simulators only where uncertainty, causal relevance or out-of-distribution (OOD) conditions demand it. Each entry records what the work actually demonstrated (quantitatively where the source supports it), why it matters to the programme, where it breaks, and the concrete design consequence. Novelty threat rates how directly the work already occupies the programme's intended contribution ("direct" = someone has done a close version of the same thing; "partial" = one component overlaps; "none" = an ingredient, not a competitor).

**Verification protocol and an important caveat.** The sandbox used for this scan had a network egress allow-list that blocked *every* publisher, preprint and index domain tried (arxiv.org and mirrors, doi.org, nature.com, pnas.org, sciencedirect, royalsocietypublishing, eLife, PLOS, PMLR, NeurIPS/ICLR/ICML proceedings, OpenReview, Semantic Scholar, dblp, PubMed/PMC/EuropePMC, ADS, OSTI, JOSS, Cell, Science, IEEE, ACM, Wiley, Springer, Cambridge, AAAI OJS, Project Euclid, bioRxiv, institutional repositories and github.io pages). The only host that opened was github.com (including raw.githubusercontent.com). Therefore:

- **Verified: yes (URL)** means I actually opened the *author-maintained official code repository* for the paper and read its citation block (README or CITATION file), which stated the title, author list and venue/year (and DOI where the authors include one). Where the opened page confirmed only some fields, the entry says exactly which fields were confirmed there and which came from search-engine result snippets. This is weaker than opening the DOI/arXiv page and should be re-checked from an unrestricted network before any of these citations are reused in a manuscript.
- **Verified: citation-only (unconfirmed)** means no page could be opened. Where a search-engine result snippet (quoting the arXiv/publisher page) corroborated the bibliographic details, the entry says "search-snippet corroborated" and gives the URL the snippet came from; where the details rest on my prior knowledge only, the entry says so.
- No DOI in this file was invented. DOIs marked "(derived from article URL)" were formed from the publisher article ID shown on an opened page (e.g. Nature `s41592-025-02895-w` → `10.1038/s41592-025-02895-w`). DOIs marked "(not confirmed in this scan)" come from prior knowledge and must be re-checked.
- The search budget for this session was exhausted after roughly 45 queries, so ~10 planned corroboration searches (mostly for entries that were already repository-verified) could not be run; those entries note it.

Entries are grouped by thread. The count (53) exceeds the nominal 30–45 target because the brief names roughly 45 specific works plus their known criticisms and follow-ups; closely related works are merged into single entries where sensible rather than dropped, and nothing was added beyond what the brief asked for.

## Thread 1 — Neural operators

### fno-2021 — Li, Kovachki, Azizzadenesheli, Liu, Bhattacharya, Stuart, Anandkumar (2021). Fourier Neural Operator for Parametric Partial Differential Equations. ICLR 2021. arXiv:2010.08895 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.08895)
- **Key result:** Parameterises the integral kernel of a neural operator in Fourier space, learning resolution-invariant maps between function spaces (e.g. initial condition → solution). The paper reports zero-shot super-resolution (train at 64², evaluate at 256²) and up to three orders of magnitude speed-up over a conventional pseudo-spectral solver on 2-D Navier–Stokes; on the hardest turbulent case (viscosity 1e-5, from memory of Table 1) FNO-3D's relative L2 error is ≈0.19, i.e. usable but far from solver accuracy. The companion journal paper (Kovachki et al. 2023, *JMLR* 24(1):89, arXiv:2108.08481 — verified via the neuraloperator README) gives the general neural-operator framework and universal-approximation results.
- **Relevance:** The obvious candidate for the "fast surrogate" tier that the hierarchical world model consults before deciding whether to call a mechanistic solver; resolution invariance matters because biological fine-scale simulators (reaction–diffusion, tissue mechanics) run on varying meshes.
- **Limitations:** Periodic/regular-grid bias of the FFT (irregular geometries need GINO/geometry-aware variants); errors compound in autoregressive rollouts (see mp-pde-2022); accuracy degrades sharply OOD in PDE coefficients/forcing (see Subramanian et al., in mp-pde-2022); no built-in uncertainty; needs thousands of solver trajectories for training, which is exactly the expensive regime the programme wants to avoid.
- **Design implication for Physics-to-Life:** Use FNO/neural-operator layers only as the *lowest-fidelity* tier with an explicit trust region defined by training-distribution coverage; never let an operator rollout run more than a few steps without a check against a mechanistic evaluation or a calibrated error bound; plan on a data-efficiency problem, not just an architecture choice.
- **Novelty threat:** none — FNO is an ingredient; it does not decide *when* to consult higher fidelity.
- **Verified:** citation-only (unconfirmed) — search-snippet corroborated (title, full author list, ICLR 2021, arXiv id; dblp record https://dblp.org/rec/conf/iclr/LiKALBSA21.html appeared in results). The official repository (https://github.com/zongyi-li/fourier_neural_operator) now redirects to https://github.com/neuraloperator/neuraloperator, whose README cites the JMLR 2023 framework paper but no longer the ICLR paper.

### deeponet-2021 — Lu, Jin, Pang, Zhang, Karniadakis (2021). Learning nonlinear operators via DeepONet based on the universal approximation theorem of operators. Nature Machine Intelligence 3(3):218–229. DOI 10.1038/s42256-021-00302-5
- **Key result:** Branch/trunk architecture grounded in Chen & Chen's (1995) universal approximation theorem for operators. Learns explicit operators (integrals, fractional derivatives) and implicit ones (ODE/PDE solution operators); shows generalisation error far below plain fully connected networks and characterises regimes of the error vs. training-set size (an initial fast-decay phase followed by polynomial decay).
- **Relevance:** Query points are arbitrary (trunk net), so DeepONet is natural for sparse, irregular biological observations and for mapping intervention functions (dose schedules, boundary forcing) to responses.
- **Limitations:** Fixed sensor locations for the branch input; accuracy and data needs scale poorly with input-function dimensionality; like FNO it has no uncertainty and is silent about OOD inputs; the "physics-informed DeepONet" variants inherit PINN optimisation pathologies.
- **Design implication for Physics-to-Life:** Prefer DeepONet-style trunk nets for intervention→response maps where queries are irregular; couple every operator output to a UQ head (deep ensemble or conformal set) before it can influence routing.
- **Novelty threat:** none.
- **Verified:** yes (https://github.com/lululxvi/deeponet — README citation gives title, authors, journal, volume, pages, DOI).

### pino-2021 — Li, Zheng, Kovachki, Jin, Chen, Liu, Azizzadenesheli, Anandkumar (2021). Physics-Informed Neural Operator for Learning Partial Differential Equations. arXiv:2111.03794 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.03794)
- **Key result:** Combines operator learning (data at low resolution) with PDE-residual losses (evaluated at higher resolution via exact Fourier differentiation), so the operator can be trained with few or no solver trajectories and fine-tuned per instance at test time; reported to close much of the gap between FNO and the numerical solver on Kolmogorov flow and to work in regimes where PINNs fail to converge. A journal version (ACM/IMS Journal of Data Science, 2024) exists to my knowledge but was not confirmed in this scan.
- **Relevance:** Directly addresses the programme's "simulator as teacher and constraint" idea: physics residuals let mechanistic knowledge supervise the learned model where data are scarce.
- **Limitations:** Requires the governing equations in closed form (the missing-physics case is exactly where PINO has nothing to say); residual losses assume the equations are correct, so model-form error is silently absorbed; test-time fine-tuning costs solver-like compute.
- **Design implication for Physics-to-Life:** Use PDE residuals as a *soft* constraint only on the parts of the biology whose physics is trusted (transport, conservation laws); route model-form-uncertain parts to closure learning (Thread 2) instead.
- **Novelty threat:** partial — PINO already uses physics as a training constraint for operators, but not adaptively or with uncertainty-driven fidelity selection.
- **Verified:** yes (https://github.com/neuraloperator/physics_informed — README gives title, full author list, 2021, arXiv id).

### mp-pde-2022 — Brandstetter, Worrall, Welling (2022). Message Passing Neural PDE Solvers. ICLR 2022 (spotlight). arXiv:2202.03376 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.03376)
- **Key result:** Graph-based autoregressive neural PDE solver that generalises across resolutions, time steps and boundary conditions; introduces the "pushforward trick" (adversarial-style training on the model's own one-step outputs) and temporal bundling to fight error accumulation in long rollouts, with stable rollouts far beyond the training horizon on 1-D/2-D benchmarks. Two related works could not be page-verified in this scan but are the standard references for the same limitation: PDE-Refiner (Lippe, Veeling, Perdikaris, Turner, Brandstetter, NeurIPS 2023, arXiv:2308.05732 — prior knowledge) shows that neural solvers ignore low-amplitude spectral components and that iterative denoising-style refinement restores accurate long rollouts; Subramanian et al. (NeurIPS 2023, arXiv:2306.00258 — prior knowledge; their repository https://github.com/ShashankSubramanian/neuraloperators-TL-scaling was opened but its README carries no citation) quantify how neural-operator error grows OOD in PDE coefficients and how pre-training/fine-tuning partially recovers it.
- **Relevance:** The programme's world model must roll out over long biological horizons (days–years of ageing); error accumulation and unmodelled spectral content are the failure mode most likely to make a learned rollout diverge from the mechanistic truth.
- **Limitations:** Pushforward training stabilises but does not certify; residual error still grows; results are on low-dimensional canonical PDEs, not stiff, multiscale biochemistry.
- **Design implication for Physics-to-Life:** Treat rollout length as a fidelity knob: train with pushforward/temporal bundling, monitor a rollout-drift statistic (spectral energy in high modes, ensemble spread) and trigger a mechanistic re-anchoring step when it exceeds a calibrated threshold. This "re-anchor when drifting" loop is a natural place for the programme's routing policy.
- **Novelty threat:** partial — long-rollout stabilisation is well studied; adaptive re-anchoring against a mechanistic solver is not.
- **Verified:** yes (https://github.com/brandstetter-johannes/MP-Neural-PDE-Solvers — README gives title, authors, ICLR 2022, arXiv id). PDE-Refiner and Subramanian et al.: citation-only (unconfirmed).

## Thread 2 — Closure modelling with ML

### gupta-lermusiaux-2021 — Gupta, Lermusiaux (2021). Neural closure models for dynamical systems. Proceedings of the Royal Society A 477:20201004. DOI 10.1098/rspa.2020.1004 (arXiv:2012.13869)
- **Key result:** Learns non-Markovian closure terms for truncated/coarsened known-physics models by augmenting them with neural delay differential equations (nDDEs), motivated by the Mori–Zwanzig formalism (memory terms are exact consequences of coarse-graining). Trained through the solver (adjoint of the DDE), using high-fidelity simulations as the teacher, on advecting-shock, ocean-acidification/biogeochemical and turbulence examples; the memory-carrying closure outperforms Markovian neural closures.
- **Relevance:** This is the closest existing formalisation of the programme's "learned model handles closure terms / missing physics, mechanistic model is the teacher" idea, applied to biology-adjacent (biogeochemical) systems.
- **Limitations:** Requires a differentiable low-fidelity model; nDDE training is expensive and sensitive to the delay horizon; no uncertainty on the closure; no mechanism for deciding when the closure is untrustworthy.
- **Design implication for Physics-to-Life:** Adopt the Mori–Zwanzig framing explicitly: closures at each hierarchy level should carry memory (state history or latent summaries) rather than be instantaneous functions of the coarse state; budget for solver-in-the-loop (adjoint) training.
- **Novelty threat:** partial — the closure-learning half of the programme is well covered here; the fidelity-routing half is not.
- **Verified:** citation-only (unconfirmed) — search-snippet corroborated (Royal Society page https://royalsocietypublishing.org/doi/10.1098/rspa.2020.1004 and arXiv listing appeared in results with matching title/authors/volume/article number).

### sanderse-closure-review-2024 — Sanderse, Stinis, Maulik, Ahmed (2024). Scientific machine learning for closure models in multiscale problems: a review. Foundations of Data Science 7(1). DOI 10.3934/fods.2024043 (arXiv:2403.02913)
- **Key result:** Systematises ML closures by (i) how much known physics the reduced model retains, (ii) a priori (offline, fit the closure term) vs a posteriori (online, solver-in-the-loop) learning objectives, and (iii) structure preservation (conservation, dissipation, symmetries). The central message: a priori accuracy does not imply a posteriori stability, and stable closures generally need either solver-in-the-loop training or structural constraints.
- **Relevance:** Provides the taxonomy the programme should use to specify each learned closure: what physics is kept, what objective it is trained with, and which invariants are enforced.
- **Limitations:** Fluid-mechanics-centric; biological multiscale problems (stochastic, discrete, hybrid) are barely represented; stochastic closures are treated briefly.
- **Design implication for Physics-to-Life:** Default to a posteriori (through-the-solver) training with structure-preserving parameterisations (mass/charge conservation, positivity of concentrations, thermodynamic consistency) for every closure; reserve a priori fitting for initialisation only.
- **Novelty threat:** none — it is a review.
- **Verified:** citation-only (unconfirmed) — search-snippet corroborated (AIMS journal page https://www.aimsciences.org/article/doi/10.3934/fods.2024043 and arXiv 2403.02913 appeared in results; authors, venue and DOI matched). The AIMS and CWI repository pages could not be opened.

### kochkov-2021 — Kochkov, Smith, Alieva, Wang, Brenner, Hoyer (2021). Machine learning–accelerated computational fluid dynamics. PNAS 118(21):e2101784118. DOI 10.1073/pnas.2101784118
- **Key result:** Learned interpolation/correction components embedded inside a differentiable finite-volume solver (JAX-CFD) and trained end-to-end through the solver on 2-D turbulence. Reported accuracy equals baseline solvers at 8–10× finer resolution per spatial dimension, giving 40–80× speed-ups, with generalisation to forcing, Reynolds number and domain sizes not seen in training (numbers from the abstract as quoted in search results).
- **Relevance:** The canonical demonstration that "learned components inside a mechanistic solver, trained through the solver" is stable and generalises better than replacing the solver outright — the same architecture pattern the programme wants at each hierarchy level.
- **Limitations:** Requires a differentiable solver; long rollouts still drift; gains shrink for 3-D and stiff systems; no uncertainty estimate to tell when the learned correction has left its comfort zone.
- **Design implication for Physics-to-Life:** Build the hierarchy on differentiable mechanistic cores (Thread 4) so corrections can be trained through them; measure equivalent-resolution gains as the primary success metric for each learned component.
- **Novelty threat:** partial — hybrid solver-in-the-loop learning is established; adaptive fidelity routing on top of it is not.
- **Verified:** yes (https://github.com/google/jax-cfd — README gives title, full author list, PNAS 2021, 118(21), DOI). Quantitative claims corroborated by the PubMed/PNAS search snippet.

### stachenfeld-2022 — Stachenfeld, Fielding, Kochkov, Cranmer, Pfaff, Godwin, Cui, Ho, Battaglia, Sanchez-Gonzalez (2022). Learned Coarse Models for Efficient Turbulence Simulation. ICLR 2022. arXiv:2112.15275 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.15275)
- **Key result:** Fully learned (non-hybrid) coarse simulators trained end-to-end at low spatial and temporal resolution reproduce turbulent statistics more accurately than classical solvers run at the same coarse resolution, including Athena++ astrophysical MHD trajectories; stability of long rollouts depends on training tricks (noise injection, as in the graph-network simulator of Sanchez-Gonzalez et al., ICML 2020, arXiv:2002.09405 — verified via https://github.com/google-deepmind/deepmind-research/tree/master/learning_to_simulate).
- **Relevance:** Defines the opposite pole from Kochkov: replace rather than augment the solver. Useful as the "cheap coarse model" tier, and as evidence that learned coarse models need explicit stabilisation.
- **Limitations:** No physics guarantees; generalisation across regimes is weaker than the hybrid approach; training needs many high-resolution trajectories; failures are silent.
- **Design implication for Physics-to-Life:** Allow pure learned coarse models only where a mechanistic fallback exists and is cheap enough to be sampled for spot-checks; adopt noise-injection training for any autoregressive coarse model.
- **Novelty threat:** none.
- **Verified:** citation-only (unconfirmed) — search-snippet corroborated (arXiv listing with full author list; DeepMind publication page in results). No official repository was found.

### learned-les-closures-2019 — Beck, Flad, Munz (2019). Deep neural networks for data-driven LES closure models. Journal of Computational Physics 398:108910. (Companion: Maulik, San, Rasheed, Vedula (2019). Subgrid modelling for two-dimensional turbulence using neural networks. Journal of Fluid Mechanics 858:122–144.)
- **Key result:** Beck et al. train CNN closures on DNS of decaying isotropic turbulence and obtain high a priori correlation with the exact subgrid terms, but report that directly inserting the network into an LES is unstable; the usable closure had to be re-cast as an eddy-viscosity-type model informed by the network. Maulik et al. likewise obtain good a priori prediction of the Kraichnan-turbulence source term from local stencils and need stabilising post-processing in a posteriori deployment.
- **Relevance:** The clearest early evidence of the "a priori accurate, a posteriori unstable" trap that any learned closure in the programme will face.
- **Limitations:** Fluid-specific; stabilisation heuristics are ad hoc; no theory of when instability appears.
- **Design implication for Physics-to-Life:** Never certify a closure on offline (a priori) error alone; require rollout (a posteriori) tests against the mechanistic teacher, and prefer dissipative/structure-preserving closure forms.
- **Novelty threat:** none.
- **Verified:** citation-only (unconfirmed) — search-snippet corroborated for both (ADS/ResearchGate/OSTI/Cambridge listings with matching titles, authors, journals, volumes and pages).

### kurz-beck-rl-closure-2023 — Kurz, Offenhäuser, Beck (2023). Deep reinforcement learning for turbulence modeling in large eddy simulations. International Journal of Heat and Fluid Flow 99:109094. DOI 10.1016/j.ijheatfluidflow.2022.109094 (Companion: Beck, Kurz (2023). Toward discretization-consistent closure schemes for large eddy simulation using reinforcement learning. Physics of Fluids 35(12):125122. DOI 10.1063/5.0176223)
- **Key result:** Trains the closure as a policy with RL inside the running LES (the Relexi framework), optimising a posteriori statistics directly; the resulting closures are stable by construction of the objective and adapt to the discretisation, avoiding the a priori/a posteriori mismatch of supervised closures. No differentiable solver is required.
- **Relevance:** Offers a route to a posteriori closure training when the mechanistic simulator is *not* differentiable (common for biological agent-based or stochastic simulators).
- **Limitations:** RL sample inefficiency (many LES rollouts); reward design determines what "stable" means; results are for canonical turbulence only.
- **Design implication for Physics-to-Life:** Keep RL-through-the-simulator as the fallback training route for non-differentiable mechanistic modules; use it also for the routing policy itself (see Thread 10).
- **Novelty threat:** partial — RL is used here to train closures, not to allocate fidelity.
- **Verified:** yes (https://github.com/flexi-framework/relexi — README lists both papers with authors, journals, volumes, article numbers and DOIs).

## Thread 3 — Hybrid mechanistic + neural models / missing physics

### ude-2020 — Rackauckas, Ma, Martensen, Warner, Zubov, Supekar, Skinner, Ramadhan, Edelman (2020). Universal Differential Equations for Scientific Machine Learning. arXiv:2001.04385 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.04385)
- **Key result:** Frames "known mechanistic ODE/PDE/SDE + universal approximator for the unknown terms" as one differentiable object trained with adjoint sensitivities in the SciML (Julia) ecosystem; demonstrates recovering missing interaction terms (e.g. Lotka–Volterra), then sparsifying the learned term with SINDy to a symbolic expression, plus stiff, delay and stochastic variants and high-dimensional HJB examples.
- **Relevance:** The de facto standard for "missing physics" learning; the two-stage UDE→symbolic recovery loop is exactly what the programme needs to turn learned closures back into interpretable mechanism.
- **Limitations:** Identifiability of the neural term is not guaranteed (the network can absorb errors elsewhere); training is sensitive to initialisation and requires multiple-shooting/regularisation for long or chaotic trajectories; uncertainty is not part of the formalism.
- **Design implication for Physics-to-Life:** Make UDE the default representation for each hierarchy level's dynamics (mechanistic skeleton + learned residual); pair with structural-identifiability analysis and multiple-shooting training from the start.
- **Novelty threat:** partial — UDEs provide the missing-physics component; they do not address hierarchy or fidelity routing.
- **Verified:** yes, partial (https://github.com/ChrisRackauckas/universal_differential_equations — README confirms title and arXiv id; the author list was corroborated by the arXiv search snippet).

### neural-ode-2018 — Chen, Rubanova, Bettencourt, Duvenaud (2018). Neural Ordinary Differential Equations. NeurIPS 2018. arXiv:1806.07366
- **Key result:** Continuous-depth models trained with the adjoint sensitivity method (constant memory in depth, adaptive-step solvers as layers); introduces continuous normalising flows and a latent-ODE generative model for irregularly sampled time series (extended in Rubanova, Chen, Duvenaud 2019, arXiv:1907.03907 — verified via https://github.com/YuliaRubanova/latent_ode).
- **Relevance:** The mathematical backbone for learned latent dynamics and for hidden-state inference from irregular biological sampling (clinic visits, sparse omics time points).
- **Limitations:** Adjoint gradients can be inaccurate for stiff/chaotic systems; solver cost is data-dependent and can explode; no built-in physics; latent ODEs provide encoder uncertainty but not model-form uncertainty.
- **Design implication for Physics-to-Life:** Use latent ODE/SDE encoders for hidden-state inference with irregular data, but train with checkpointed/discretise-then-optimise gradients for stiff biology rather than pure adjoints.
- **Novelty threat:** none.
- **Verified:** yes (https://github.com/rtqichen/torchdiffeq — README gives title, authors, NeurIPS 2018).

### sbinn-2020 — Yazdani, Lu, Raissi, Karniadakis (2020). Systems biology informed deep learning for inferring parameters and hidden dynamics. PLoS Computational Biology 16(11):e1007575. DOI 10.1371/journal.pcbi.1007575
- **Key result:** PINN-style networks constrained by systems-biology ODEs infer unknown kinetic parameters and *unobserved species trajectories* from sparse, noisy measurements of a subset of species, demonstrated on yeast glycolysis, a cell-apoptosis model and an ultradian glucose–insulin model; includes a structural/practical identifiability check before inference.
- **Relevance:** The most direct precedent for hidden-state inference in biological ODE systems; shows what is recoverable when the mechanistic structure is trusted.
- **Limitations:** Inherits PINN optimisation pathologies (stiffness, loss balancing); assumes the ODE structure is correct — model misspecification is not handled; point estimates only (no posterior).
- **Design implication for Physics-to-Life:** Use SBINN-type constrained inference as a baseline for hidden-state recovery, but prefer amortised Bayesian inference (Thread 6) for the production path, with identifiability screening as a mandatory pre-step.
- **Novelty threat:** none.
- **Verified:** yes (https://github.com/alirezayazdani1/SBINNs — README gives title, authors, journal, volume/issue, DOI and the three systems studied).

### aphynity-2021 — Yin, Le Guen, Dona, de Bézenac, Ayed, Thome, Gallinari (2021). Augmenting Physical Models with Deep Networks for Complex Dynamics Forecasting. ICLR 2021 (oral). arXiv:2010.04456
- **Key result:** Decomposes dynamics as physical model + neural residual, with the residual constrained to have minimal norm; proves existence and uniqueness of the decomposition (so the physical parameters remain interpretable rather than being absorbed by the network) and shows improved forecasting on reaction–diffusion, wave and damped-pendulum systems.
- **Relevance:** Provides the principled answer to "how much should the learned term be allowed to do" — the key identifiability question for grey-box models in the programme.
- **Limitations:** The minimal-norm principle is a modelling choice, not a truth criterion; requires a differentiable physical model; tested on small systems only.
- **Design implication for Physics-to-Life:** Adopt a norm/complexity penalty on learned residuals with the mechanistic part given priority, so that "missing physics" terms stay small and attributable; report the residual norm as a model-form-uncertainty diagnostic that can feed routing.
- **Novelty threat:** none.
- **Verified:** yes (https://github.com/yuan-yin/APHYNITY — README gives title, full author list, ICLR 2021 oral, arXiv id).

### pinn-2019 — Raissi, Perdikaris, Karniadakis (2019). Physics-informed neural networks: A deep learning framework for solving forward and inverse problems involving nonlinear partial differential equations. Journal of Computational Physics 378:686–707. DOI 10.1016/j.jcp.2018.10.045 (DOI not confirmed in this scan)
- **Key result:** Encodes PDE residuals, boundary and initial conditions as soft penalties on a neural network's automatic derivatives; solves forward problems and inverse (parameter-identification) problems from scattered data on Burgers, Schrödinger, Navier–Stokes and other canonical equations.
- **Relevance:** Foundational for physics-as-constraint; the inverse-problem mode is a simple route to parameter/hidden-state inference when equations are known.
- **Limitations:** See krishnapriyan-2021: fails on convection-/reaction-dominated regimes; loss balancing is fragile; cost scales poorly vs. classical solvers for forward problems; no UQ.
- **Design implication for Physics-to-Life:** Do not use PINNs as forward solvers; use them, if at all, only for small inverse problems with known structure, and prefer operator/UDE approaches elsewhere.
- **Novelty threat:** none.
- **Verified:** yes (https://github.com/maziarraissi/PINNs — README gives title, authors, journal, volume, pages, year; DOI not shown on the page).

### krishnapriyan-2021 — Krishnapriyan, Gholami, Zhe, Kirby, Mahoney (2021). Characterizing possible failure modes in physics-informed neural networks. NeurIPS 2021 (Advances in NeurIPS 34:26548–26560). arXiv:2109.01050
- **Key result:** Shows PINNs fail to learn even simple 1-D convection, reaction and reaction–diffusion problems as the PDE coefficient grows, and traces the failure to the soft-constraint loss producing an ill-conditioned optimisation landscape rather than to expressivity. Curriculum regularisation and sequence-to-sequence (time-marching) training reduce error by 1–2 orders of magnitude.
- **Relevance:** Defines when physics-as-soft-loss is unusable — directly applicable to stiff, transport-dominated biological PDEs.
- **Limitations:** Remedies are heuristics; analysis covers a few 1-D problems.
- **Design implication for Physics-to-Life:** Any physics-residual loss in the programme must be accompanied by a conditioning check; default to time-marching (sequential) training and hard-constraint formulations.
- **Novelty threat:** none.
- **Verified:** yes (https://github.com/a1k12/characterizing-pinns-failure-modes — README gives title, authors, NeurIPS 2021, arXiv id and the main findings; page numbers from the search snippet).

## Thread 4 — Differentiable simulators

### jax-md-2020 — Schoenholz, Cubuk (2020). JAX M.D.: A Framework for Differentiable Physics. NeurIPS 2020 (Advances in NeurIPS 33). (Companion: Freeman, Frey, Raichuk, Girgin, Mordatch, Bachem (2021). Brax — A Differentiable Physics Engine for Large Scale Rigid Body Simulation. NeurIPS 2021 Datasets & Benchmarks. arXiv:2106.13281)
- **Key result:** JAX-MD exposes molecular-dynamics primitives (neighbour lists, energy functions, integrators, minimisers) as pure JAX functions, so energies, forces and whole trajectories are differentiable with respect to parameters and initial conditions, and neural-network potentials plug in as ordinary energy functions. Brax does the same for rigid-body dynamics with batched simulation on accelerators (millions of environment steps per second on TPU, per the paper), enabling analytic-gradient policy optimisation.
- **Relevance:** Establishes the engineering pattern the programme needs for its mechanistic cores: simulators written as pure, vectorisable, differentiable functions so that closures, parameters and interventions can be optimised through them and batched for ensembles.
- **Limitations:** Gradients through long or chaotic trajectories explode (see metz-2021); memory grows with trajectory length unless checkpointed; neither covers stochastic/discrete biology (agent-based, Gillespie) without custom relaxations.
- **Design implication for Physics-to-Life:** Write or wrap every mechanistic module in JAX (or an equivalent) from day one; batch dimension = ensemble member; use gradient clipping/truncated horizons by default.
- **Novelty threat:** none.
- **Verified:** yes (https://github.com/google/jax-md — README citation: title, authors, NeurIPS 2020, vol. 33; https://github.com/google/brax — README citation: title, authors, NeurIPS 2021 D&B track, arXiv id).

### difftaichi-2020 — Hu, Anderson, Li, Sun, Carr, Ragan-Kelley, Durand (2020). DiffTaichi: Differentiable Programming for Physical Simulation. ICLR 2020. arXiv:1910.00935
- **Key result:** A source-to-source automatic-differentiation compiler for imperative physical simulators (MPM, FEM, fluids, rigid bodies), demonstrated on ten differentiable simulators used for gradient-based control and system identification; the paper reports code several times shorter than hand-written CUDA at comparable speed and orders of magnitude faster than a TensorFlow reimplementation (exact ratios from memory of the abstract: ≈4.2× shorter, ≈188× faster than TF).
- **Relevance:** Shows that continuum-mechanics simulators relevant to tissue/organ scale (soft-body, fluids) can be made differentiable without rewriting them in an ML framework.
- **Limitations:** Contact/discontinuities produce non-smooth or misleading gradients; differentiating through many time steps requires checkpointing and careful memory management; gradients of chaotic dynamics are unreliable.
- **Design implication for Physics-to-Life:** Prefer differentiable tissue-mechanics/transport cores built with Taichi-like or JAX tooling; treat contact-heavy or event-driven modules as non-differentiable and train their closures via RL or gradient-free methods.
- **Novelty threat:** none.
- **Verified:** yes (https://github.com/taichi-dev/difftaichi — README citation: title, full author list, ICLR 2020, arXiv id).

### jaxley-2025 — Deistler, Kadhim, Pals, Beck, Huang, Gloeckler, Lappalainen, Schröder, Berens, Gonçalves, Macke (2025). Jaxley: differentiable simulation enables large-scale training of detailed biophysical models of neural dynamics. Nature Methods (2025). https://www.nature.com/articles/s41592-025-02895-w (DOI 10.1038/s41592-025-02895-w, derived from article URL)
- **Key result:** A JAX-based differentiable simulator for multicompartment Hodgkin–Huxley-type neurons and networks (GPU/TPU, JIT, vectorised over parameters). Because gradients are available, biophysical models with very many free parameters (channel densities, synapses across compartments and cells) can be fitted to data or trained on tasks by gradient descent instead of gradient-free search, which previously limited fits to tens of parameters.
- **Relevance:** The strongest existing example of "make the mechanistic biological simulator differentiable and train through it" — precisely the teacher/constraint role the programme envisions, in a domain (neuroscience) where mechanistic models are trusted.
- **Limitations:** Deterministic conductance-based models only; gradients through spiking, long simulations require care (loss smoothing, truncation); no coupling to slower processes (gene expression, ageing) that the programme cares about.
- **Design implication for Physics-to-Life:** Use Jaxley as a reference mechanistic tier for any neural/excitable-tissue component and as a template for how other biological simulators (cardiac, metabolic) should be re-implemented.
- **Novelty threat:** partial — differentiable biophysical simulation is done; hierarchical coupling with learned coarse models and uncertainty-routed queries is not.
- **Verified:** yes (https://github.com/jaxleyverse/jaxley — README citation: title, author list, Nature Methods 2025, article URL). The author list beyond Gonçalves is abbreviated as "and others" in the README; Macke as senior author from prior knowledge. The planned corroborating search could not be run (budget exhausted).

### alquraishi-sorger-2021 — AlQuraishi, Sorger (2021). Differentiable biology: using deep learning for biophysics-based and data-driven modeling of molecular mechanisms. Nature Methods 18:1169–1180. DOI 10.1038/s41592-021-01283-4
- **Key result:** A perspective arguing that differentiable programs combining domain equations with learnable components can span from single assays to protein folding, and that the "differentiable biology" stack (end-to-end differentiability, learned components where mechanism is unknown) is the right abstraction for mechanistic modelling with modern data.
- **Relevance:** Articulates the programme's philosophy at the molecular level and provides a citable framing for "mechanistic models as differentiable teachers".
- **Limitations:** Conceptual; no benchmarks; molecular focus (no multiscale hierarchy, no fidelity allocation).
- **Design implication for Physics-to-Life:** Cite as motivation; do not expect method guidance beyond the differentiable-program pattern.
- **Novelty threat:** none.
- **Verified:** citation-only (unconfirmed) — search-snippet corroborated (Nature Methods page https://www.nature.com/articles/s41592-021-01283-4 and PubMed 34608321 in results with matching authors, volume, pages, DOI).

### metz-2021 — Metz, Freeman, Schoenholz, Kachman (2021). Gradients are Not All You Need. arXiv:2111.05803 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.05803)
- **Key result:** Identifies a common failure of differentiable programming across recurrent nets, physics simulation and learned optimisers: when the system's Jacobian spectrum implies chaotic sensitivity, exact gradients of long-horizon objectives have exploding variance and become useless even though the loss is smooth; gives criteria (from the Jacobian spectrum) for when to expect failure and suggests black-box/evolutionary or truncated-gradient alternatives.
- **Relevance:** Biological dynamics (oscillators, excitable media, chaotic population dynamics) are exactly where naive differentiation through the mechanistic simulator will fail; this determines when the programme must fall back to gradient-free or short-horizon training.
- **Limitations:** Diagnostic rather than a fix; the criteria require Jacobian estimates that are themselves expensive.
- **Design implication for Physics-to-Life:** Instrument every differentiable simulator with a Lyapunov/Jacobian-spectrum diagnostic; when positive, train closures on short windows, use multiple shooting, or switch to RL/ES; expose this diagnostic to the routing policy as a "gradient reliability" signal.
- **Novelty threat:** none.
- **Verified:** citation-only (unconfirmed) — search-snippet corroborated (arXiv listing with matching authors and abstract).

## Thread 5 — Uncertainty quantification in SciML

### deep-ensembles-2017 — Lakshminarayanan, Pritzel, Blundell (2017). Simple and Scalable Predictive Uncertainty Estimation using Deep Ensembles. NeurIPS 2017, pp. 6402–6413. arXiv:1612.01474
- **Key result:** Training M (typically 5) randomly initialised networks with a proper scoring rule (e.g. Gaussian NLL for regression) plus optional adversarial-example smoothing gives predictive uncertainty as good as or better than approximate Bayesian NNs on classification/regression, with better calibration under dataset shift and trivial parallelism.
- **Relevance:** The most robust, least-assumption baseline for epistemic uncertainty on learned surrogates and closures, and the one most likely to survive OOD shift — which is what routing decisions depend on.
- **Limitations:** M× training/inference cost; ensembles agree confidently on some OOD inputs (no coverage guarantee); disagreement measures a mixture of epistemic and aleatoric effects unless heteroscedastic heads are used; not applicable to the mechanistic solver itself.
- **Design implication for Physics-to-Life:** Make deep ensembles (heteroscedastic heads, M≈5) the default epistemic signal for every learned component; use ensemble disagreement as the primary trigger for querying the mechanistic tier, then wrap it in conformal calibration (below) for guarantees.
- **Novelty threat:** none.
- **Verified:** citation-only (unconfirmed) — search-snippet corroborated (NeurIPS proceedings page https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2017/hash/9ef2ed4b7fd2c810847ffa5fa85bce38-Abstract.html and arXiv 1612.01474 in results with matching authors and pages).

### psaros-uq-review-2023 — Psaros, Meng, Zou, Guo, Karniadakis (2023). Uncertainty quantification in scientific machine learning: Methods, metrics, and comparisons. Journal of Computational Physics 477:111902. DOI 10.1016/j.jcp.2022.111902
- **Key result:** Systematic comparison of UQ methods (Bayesian NNs via HMC/VI/Laplace, deep ensembles, MC dropout, functional priors, GANs) applied to PINNs, neural operators and stochastic PDE solvers, across regression, forward/inverse PDE and stochastic problems, with a unified set of metrics and an accompanying library (NeuralUQ, https://github.com/Crunch-UQ4MI/neuraluq). Broad finding: no single method dominates; ensembles and HMC-based BNNs are the most reliable, cheap approximations are often miscalibrated, and separating aleatoric from epistemic/model-form error matters most in the PDE setting.
- **Relevance:** The reference taxonomy and benchmark set for choosing UQ for operators/PINNs, i.e. for the learned tiers of the programme.
- **Limitations:** Small canonical PDEs; no OOD/shift protocol; conformal methods are absent; metrics assume ground truth is available.
- **Design implication for Physics-to-Life:** Reuse the review's metric suite (calibration, sharpness, interval coverage) but add explicit shift tests; treat "model-form" uncertainty as a separate, tracked quantity because it is what fidelity routing must respond to.
- **Novelty threat:** none.
- **Verified:** citation-only (unconfirmed) — search-snippet corroborated (OSTI, ACM DL and ScienceDirect listings with matching authors, volume, article number, DOI). The NeuralUQ README (opened) links the same ScienceDirect article.

### conformal-2021 — Angelopoulos, Bates (2021). A Gentle Introduction to Conformal Prediction and Distribution-Free Uncertainty Quantification. arXiv:2107.07511 (later published in Foundations and Trends in Machine Learning, 2023 — not confirmed in this scan)
- **Key result:** Split conformal prediction converts any heuristic uncertainty score into prediction sets with finite-sample marginal coverage 1−α under exchangeability, using only a held-out calibration set; covers regression intervals, conformalised quantile regression, risk control and adaptive sets. For simulators/surrogates, recent work applies it to neural operators (Ma, Pitt, Azizzadenesheli, Anandkumar, TMLR 2024, "Calibrated uncertainty quantification for operator learning via conformal prediction" — search-snippet only) and to general surrogate models (Gopakumar et al. 2024, arXiv:2408.09881 — search-snippet only; authors not confirmed).
- **Relevance:** The only UQ family that gives guarantees without trusting the learned model — exactly what a routing rule ("query the simulator when the conformal interval is wider than tolerance") should be built on.
- **Limitations:** Marginal, not conditional, coverage; guarantees vanish under distribution shift (the OOD case the programme most cares about) unless weighted/adaptive variants are used; sets can be wide/uninformative; function-valued outputs need care (pointwise vs. functional coverage).
- **Design implication for Physics-to-Life:** Calibrate every learned tier's error score conformally on held-out mechanistic simulations; treat coverage failure detected online (via mechanistic spot checks) as a shift alarm that forces re-calibration and higher-fidelity routing.
- **Novelty threat:** partial — conformal UQ for surrogates exists; using it as the decision variable for fidelity allocation does not appear to.
- **Verified:** yes (https://github.com/aangelopoulos/conformal-prediction — README citation: title, authors, arXiv 2021). Ma et al. 2024 and Gopakumar et al. 2024: citation-only (unconfirmed).

### guo-calibration-2017 — Guo, Pleiss, Sun, Weinberger (2017). On Calibration of Modern Neural Networks. ICML 2017 (PMLR 70). arXiv:1706.04599
- **Key result:** Introduces reliability diagrams and expected calibration error (ECE) as the standard diagnostics; shows modern deep nets are systematically over-confident (depth, width, batch-norm, weak weight decay worsen it) and that single-parameter temperature scaling on a validation set fixes most of it for classification.
- **Relevance:** Supplies the calibration vocabulary (ECE, reliability diagrams) the programme must report; the regression analogue (Kuleshov, Fenner, Ermon, ICML 2018, "Accurate uncertainties for deep learning using calibrated regression" — not verified in this scan) uses quantile-based calibration curves.
- **Limitations:** ECE is binning-dependent and can be gamed; post-hoc scaling does not fix OOD over-confidence; classification-centric.
- **Design implication for Physics-to-Life:** Report both calibration (ECE/quantile calibration) and sharpness for every uncertainty signal that drives routing; never rely on post-hoc temperature scaling alone for OOD detection.
- **Novelty threat:** none.
- **Verified:** citation-only (unconfirmed) — search-snippet corroborated (PMLR page https://proceedings.mlr.press/v70/guo17a.html and arXiv in results); the co-author-maintained repository https://github.com/gpleiss/temperature_scaling (opened) links arXiv 1706.04599 but does not list authors/venue.

### evidential-regression-2020 — Amini, Schwarting, Soleimany, Rus (2020). Deep Evidential Regression. NeurIPS 2020 (Advances in NeurIPS 33). Criticisms: Bengs, Hüllermeier, Waegeman (2022). Pitfalls of Epistemic Uncertainty Quantification through Loss Minimisation. NeurIPS 2022, arXiv:2203.06102; Meinert, Gawlikowski, Lavin (2023). The Unreasonable Effectiveness of Deep Evidential Regression. AAAI 2023, pp. 9134–9142, arXiv:2205.10060, DOI 10.1609/aaai.v37i8.26096
- **Key result:** Amini et al. place a Normal-Inverse-Gamma prior over the Gaussian likelihood parameters and train a single network to output its hyperparameters, yielding aleatoric and "epistemic" uncertainty in one forward pass, with OOD inputs producing low evidence. Bengs et al. prove that minimising such second-order losses is not a proper way to learn epistemic uncertainty (the learner is not incentivised to be truthful about its second-order distribution); Meinert et al. show DER's epistemic estimate is a heuristic tied to the regulariser and that the aleatoric/epistemic split is not identified, while still often being empirically useful.
- **Relevance:** Evidential heads are attractive for the programme's compute budget (one pass), but the criticisms show their "epistemic" signal is not a reliable trigger for expensive simulator queries.
- **Limitations:** As above; sensitivity to the regulariser weight; poor behaviour under shift in practice.
- **Design implication for Physics-to-Life:** Do not use evidential regression as the routing signal; if used at all, use it only as a cheap pre-filter validated against ensemble/conformal signals.
- **Novelty threat:** none.
- **Verified:** yes for Amini et al. (https://github.com/aamini/evidential-deep-learning — README citation: title, authors, NeurIPS vol. 33, 2020). Bengs et al. and Meinert et al.: citation-only (unconfirmed) — search-snippet corroborated (NeurIPS proceedings page and AAAI OJS/ACM DL listings with matching titles, authors, pages, DOI).

### laplace-redux-2021 — Daxberger, Kristiadi, Immer, Eschenhagen, Bauer, Hennig (2021). Laplace Redux — Effortless Bayesian Deep Learning. NeurIPS 2021. arXiv:2106.14806
- **Key result:** Post-hoc Laplace approximations (last-layer, subnetwork, or KFAC/diagonal full-network) turn a trained MAP network into an approximate Bayesian one at negligible extra training cost; with the marginal likelihood usable for hyperparameter selection, and calibration/OOD performance competitive with ensembles and VI on standard benchmarks. Provides the `laplace-torch` library.
- **Relevance:** Cheapest credible route to epistemic uncertainty on already-trained surrogates/closures, and to Bayesian last-layer models that can be updated online as mechanistic evidence arrives.
- **Limitations:** Local Gaussian approximation around one mode; last-layer variants capture little feature uncertainty; linearised predictives needed to avoid over-dispersion; scaling of full-network Hessians.
- **Design implication for Physics-to-Life:** Use last-layer Laplace (or Bayesian last layer with online updates) as the fast, updatable uncertainty for high-throughput components; retain deep ensembles for the components whose uncertainty gates simulator calls.
- **Novelty threat:** none.
- **Verified:** yes (https://github.com/aleximmer/Laplace — README citation: title, authors, NeurIPS 2021, arXiv id).

## Thread 6 — Simulation-based / amortised Bayesian inference

### cranmer-sbi-frontier-2020 — Cranmer, Brehmer, Louppe (2020). The frontier of simulation-based inference. PNAS 117(48):30055–30062. DOI 10.1073/pnas.1912789117 (arXiv:1911.01429)
- **Key result:** Reviews likelihood-free inference for simulators with intractable likelihoods, organising methods by what is learned (posterior, likelihood, likelihood ratio), whether inference is amortised or sequential, and whether simulator internals (gradients, latent trajectories: "mining gold") are exploited; identifies neural density estimation, active learning and simulator augmentation as the forces reshaping the field.
- **Relevance:** Provides the taxonomy for the programme's hidden-state and parameter inference layer, including the option to exploit differentiable simulators (Thread 4) for more efficient inference.
- **Limitations:** Review; predates diffusion/flow-matching estimators and most misspecification work.
- **Design implication for Physics-to-Life:** Frame hidden-state inference as amortised NPE over the mechanistic simulator, and plan from the outset to exploit simulator gradients/latents where available.
- **Novelty threat:** none.
- **Verified:** citation-only (unconfirmed) — search-snippet corroborated (PNAS page https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1912789117, PubMed 32471948 and arXiv listing with matching authors, volume, pages, DOI).

### goncalves-2020 — Gonçalves, Lueckmann, Deistler, Nonnenmacher, Öcal, Bassetto, Chintaluri, Podlaski, Haddad, Vogels, Greenberg, Macke (2020). Training deep neural density estimators to identify mechanistic models of neural dynamics. eLife 9:e56261. DOI 10.7554/eLife.56261
- **Key result:** Sequential neural posterior estimation (SNPE) with normalising flows recovers full posteriors over parameters of mechanistic neuron models — Hodgkin–Huxley single neurons (from summary features of recordings), ion-channel models, receptive-field models and the crustacean stomatogastric-ganglion pyloric circuit (31 parameters) — revealing large, structured sets of parameters consistent with data and enabling exploration of compensation among conductances.
- **Relevance:** The canonical demonstration that amortised Bayesian inference works on real mechanistic biological simulators; a template for the programme's "infer hidden parameters/state of the virtual system from sparse measurements" step.
- **Limitations:** Hand-crafted summary statistics; posterior quality unverified where the simulator is misspecified; expensive for high-dimensional state inference (it targets parameters, not time-varying hidden states).
- **Design implication for Physics-to-Life:** Use SNPE/NPE with learned (not hand-crafted) summaries for parameter inference at each hierarchy level; extend to state inference by treating latent initial conditions as parameters; always run simulation-based calibration checks.
- **Novelty threat:** none.
- **Verified:** yes (https://github.com/mackelab/IdentifyMechanisticModels_2020 — README citation: title, full author list, eLife 2020, DOI).

### npe-apt — Papamakarios, Murray (2016). Fast ε-free Inference of Simulation Models with Bayesian Conditional Density Estimation. NeurIPS 2016. arXiv:1605.06376. Greenberg, Nonnenmacher, Macke (2019). Automatic Posterior Transformation for Likelihood-free Inference. ICML 2019 (PMLR 97:2404–2414)
- **Key result:** Papamakarios & Murray introduce neural posterior estimation: train a conditional density estimator on (θ, x) pairs from the simulator to approximate p(θ|x) directly, with sequential proposal refinement (SNPE-A), avoiding ABC's rejection inefficiency. Greenberg et al. (APT/SNPE-C) make sequential NPE work with arbitrary proposals and flow-based estimators without importance-weight or analytic-correction pathologies, improving efficiency and robustness.
- **Relevance:** These are the algorithms the programme would actually run for hidden-state inference; APT is the default in the sbi toolbox.
- **Limitations:** Sequential rounds reduce amortisation; density estimators can leak (mass outside the prior); high-dimensional observations need learned embeddings; no protection against misspecification (see schmitt-misspec).
- **Design implication for Physics-to-Life:** Default to amortised NPE (single round, learned embedding nets) so inference is cheap at deployment; fall back to sequential APT only for expensive simulators with few observations.
- **Novelty threat:** none.
- **Verified:** yes for Papamakarios & Murray (https://github.com/gpapamak/epsilon_free_inference — README citation: title, authors, NeurIPS 2016, arXiv id); citation-only (unconfirmed) for Greenberg et al. — search-snippet corroborated (PMLR page https://proceedings.mlr.press/v97/greenberg19a.html and dblp record in results with matching authors, pages).

### sbi-toolbox — Tejero-Cantero, Boelts, Deistler, Lueckmann, Durkan, Gonçalves, Greenberg, Macke (2020). sbi: A toolkit for simulation-based inference. JOSS 5(52):2505. DOI 10.21105/joss.02505. Boelts, Deistler, Gloeckler, Tejero-Cantero, Lueckmann, Moss, Steinbach, Moreau, Muratore, Linhart, Durkan, Vetter, Miller, et al. (2025). sbi reloaded: a toolkit for simulation-based inference workflows. JOSS 10(108):7754. DOI 10.21105/joss.07754. (Benchmark: Lueckmann, Boelts, Greenberg, Gonçalves, Macke (2021). Benchmarking Simulation-Based Inference. AISTATS 2021, PMLR 130:343–351.)
- **Key result:** A maintained PyTorch library implementing NPE/NLE/NRE and sequential variants with flow and (in 2025) diffusion/flow-matching estimators, embedding networks, diagnostics (simulation-based calibration, coverage) and multi-round workflows. The sbibm benchmark (10 tasks with reference posteriors, C2ST metric) showed that no method dominates, that sequential methods help most for expensive simulators, and that neural methods beat ABC broadly.
- **Relevance:** The obvious implementation substrate for the programme's inference layer; sbibm gives the evaluation protocol.
- **Limitations:** Tasks are low-dimensional; no benchmark for time-varying hidden-state inference or for misspecified simulators; library assumes a black-box simulator (does not exploit gradients).
- **Design implication for Physics-to-Life:** Build the inference layer on sbi, add programme-specific benchmark tasks (hidden-state recovery under misspecification, multiscale simulators), and report C2ST/coverage as standard.

- **Verified:** yes (https://github.com/sbi-dev/sbi — README lists both JOSS citations with DOIs; https://github.com/sbi-benchmark/sbibm — README citation: title, authors, AISTATS 2021, PMLR 130:343–351).

### schmitt-misspec — Schmitt, Bürkner, Köthe, Radev (2022/2023). Detecting Model Misspecification in Amortized Bayesian Inference with Neural Networks. arXiv:2112.08866; published at DAGM GCPR 2023 (Springer LNCS, DOI 10.1007/978-3-031-54605-1_35 — search-snippet only); extended investigation arXiv:2406.03154
- **Key result:** Shows that amortised neural posterior approximators silently produce confident but wrong posteriors when observed data fall outside the simulator's generative scope, and proposes an unsupervised misspecification score based on a maximum-mean-discrepancy criterion in the learned summary space that flags such observations at inference time. Related evidence: Cannon, Ward, Schmon (2022, arXiv:2209.01845 — not verified in this scan) document large posterior errors of NPE/NLE/NRE under mild misspecification.
- **Relevance:** Misspecification is guaranteed in the programme (the mechanistic simulator is always incomplete); this gives a detector that can double as an "our simulator is missing physics here" signal to trigger closure learning or higher-fidelity queries.
- **Limitations:** Detects but does not correct; summary-space MMD has limited power for subtle misspecification; needs a reference distribution of simulator outputs.
- **Design implication for Physics-to-Life:** Instrument every amortised inference network with a summary-space misspecification score; treat alarms as evidence for missing physics (route to closure-learning/simulator-refinement), not merely as inference failures.
- **Novelty threat:** partial — misspecification detection exists; using it to drive model refinement and fidelity allocation is the programme's addition.
- **Verified:** yes for the arXiv version (https://github.com/marvinschmitt/ModelMisspecificationBF — README citation: title, authors, arXiv 2112.08866); GCPR 2023 venue and Cannon et al.: citation-only (unconfirmed).

## Thread 7 — Active learning for simulators and Bayesian experimental design

### foster-boed-2019-2021 — Foster, Jankowiak, Bingham, Horsfall, Teh, Rainforth, Goodman (2019). Variational Bayesian Optimal Experimental Design. NeurIPS 2019. arXiv:1903.05480. Foster, Ivanova, Malik, Rainforth (2021). Deep Adaptive Design: Amortizing Sequential Bayesian Experimental Design. ICML 2021 (PMLR 139). arXiv:2103.02438
- **Key result:** The 2019 paper gives fast variational bounds/estimators of expected information gain (EIG) — posterior, marginal, marginal+likelihood and variational-NMC estimators — that are orders of magnitude cheaper than nested Monte Carlo for BOED. DAD (2021) amortises *sequential* design entirely: a policy network maps the history of (design, outcome) pairs to the next design, trained offline with a contrastive lower bound on total EIG, so live experiments choose designs in milliseconds with no per-step posterior computation, matching or beating myopic non-amortised strategies.
- **Relevance:** EIG is the right currency for deciding *which* expensive mechanistic simulation (or wet-lab experiment) to run next; DAD's amortised design policy is a template for the programme's learned routing policy (a policy that decides which fidelity/module to query given the history).
- **Limitations:** EIG estimators are biased and high-variance in high dimensions; DAD assumes the simulator model is correct (misspecification corrupts EIG); policies are task-specific and need retraining per model.
- **Design implication for Physics-to-Life:** Define the routing objective as EIG (or expected reduction in decision-relevant uncertainty) per unit compute; train an amortised DAD-style policy over synthetic tasks generated by the mechanistic hierarchy; guard EIG with misspecification checks (Thread 6).
- **Novelty threat:** partial — amortised sequential design is established for experiments; applying it to allocate simulator fidelity inside a world model is the programme's angle, and needs to be positioned explicitly against DAD.
- **Verified:** yes for DAD (https://github.com/ae-foster/dad — README: title, authors, ICML paper, arXiv id); citation-only (unconfirmed) for the 2019 paper — search-snippet corroborated (OATML publication page and NeurIPS PDF https://papers.neurips.cc/paper/9553-variational-bayesian-optimal-experimental-design.pdf in results with matching author list).

### rainforth-modern-bed-2024 — Rainforth, Foster, Ivanova, Bickford Smith (2024). Modern Bayesian Experimental Design. Statistical Science 39(1):100–114. DOI 10.1214/23-STS915 (arXiv:2302.14545)
- **Key result:** Review unifying BOED, Bayesian optimisation and active learning under information-theoretic objectives; covers variational EIG estimation, gradient-based and amortised (policy) design, implicit-likelihood/simulator settings, and identifies open problems: robustness to model misspecification, non-myopic design, and design for decision-making rather than parameter learning.
- **Relevance:** Points at the exact gaps the programme lives in — design under misspecified simulators and decision-oriented (intervention-oriented) objectives.
- **Limitations:** Review; no biology-specific guidance; computational costs for large simulators are discussed only abstractly.
- **Design implication for Physics-to-Life:** Frame fidelity allocation as decision-oriented BOED (value of information for the downstream intervention decision), not as parameter-learning EIG; make misspecification robustness a stated research aim.
- **Novelty threat:** none.
- **Verified:** citation-only (unconfirmed) — search-snippet corroborated (Project Euclid page https://projecteuclid.org/journals/statistical-science/volume-39/issue-1/Modern-Bayesian-Experimental-Design/10.1214/23-STS915.short, Oxford ORA and arXiv listings with matching authors, volume, pages).

### kennedy-ohagan-2000 — Kennedy, O'Hagan (2000). Predicting the output from a complex computer code when fast approximations are available. Biometrika 87(1):1–13. DOI 10.1093/biomet/87.1.1
- **Key result:** The autoregressive multi-fidelity Gaussian-process model: the high-fidelity code is modelled as a scaled low-fidelity GP plus an independent discrepancy GP, so a few expensive runs combined with many cheap runs give a calibrated predictor with uncertainty; demonstrated on two versions of an oil-reservoir simulator. This is the root of multi-fidelity surrogates and of "fidelity as a design variable" in active learning/BO (e.g. multi-fidelity acquisition functions in BoTorch — Balandat et al., NeurIPS 2020, arXiv:1910.06403, verified via https://github.com/pytorch/botorch; the specific multi-fidelity acquisitions are from prior knowledge).
- **Relevance:** The classical statistical answer to "when is a cheap approximation enough, and how much do a few expensive runs buy": the programme's fidelity-allocation problem in its simplest form.
- **Limitations:** GP scaling (thousands of points); assumes a fixed, ordered set of fidelities and stationary discrepancy; scalar outputs; no dynamics or hierarchy of state.
- **Design implication for Physics-to-Life:** Model the learned coarse tier and the mechanistic tier as linked levels with an explicit discrepancy process; use expected reduction in discrepancy uncertainty per cost as the acquisition rule for mechanistic queries; extend to function-valued outputs with deep-kernel or operator surrogates.
- **Novelty threat:** partial — multi-fidelity GPs already allocate fidelity by uncertainty per cost; the programme's version must be dynamic, hierarchical and causal to be new.
- **Verified:** citation-only (unconfirmed) — search-snippet corroborated (Oxford Academic page https://academic.oup.com/biomet/article-abstract/87/1/1/221217 and Semantic Scholar listing with matching authors, volume, pages, DOI).

## Thread 8 — Symbolic regression / equation discovery

### sindy-2016 — Brunton, Proctor, Kutz (2016). Discovering governing equations from data by sparse identification of nonlinear dynamical systems. PNAS 113(15):3932–3937. DOI 10.1073/pnas.1517384113 (PDE extension: Rudy, Brunton, Proctor, Kutz (2017). Data-driven discovery of partial differential equations. Science Advances 3(4):e1602614)
- **Key result:** Sparse regression of estimated time derivatives onto a library of candidate functions recovers parsimonious ODEs (Lorenz, fluid wake POD models, bifurcation-parameterised systems) from data; PDE-FIND extends the library to spatial derivatives and recovers Burgers, KdV, Navier–Stokes vorticity, Schrödinger, reaction–diffusion and other PDEs from spatiotemporal data. The autoencoder variant (Champion, Lusch, Kutz, Brunton 2019, arXiv:1904.02107 — verified via https://github.com/kpchamp/SindyAutoencoders; PNAS 2019 per prior knowledge) learns coordinates in which sparse dynamics exist.
- **Relevance:** The standard tool for distilling a learned closure/UDE residual into an interpretable term, i.e. for turning "missing physics" into candidate mechanism.
- **Limitations:** Requires derivative estimates (noise-amplifying; weak-form/integral variants help), a library that contains the true terms, and observation of all relevant state variables — hidden variables, rational (Hill/Michaelis–Menten) kinetics and stochasticity, which are the norm in biology, break the vanilla method (SINDy-PI and implicit variants only partly help). Sparsity thresholds are tuned by hand and results are brittle across noise levels.
- **Design implication for Physics-to-Life:** Use SINDy-family methods only downstream of a UDE/closure fit and hidden-state inference, with weak-form regression and libraries containing rational kinetics; treat recovered equations as hypotheses to be validated against the mechanistic teacher, not as truth.
- **Novelty threat:** none.
- **Verified:** citation-only (unconfirmed) — search-snippet corroborated for both (PNAS page https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1517384113, PubMed 27035946; Science Advances page https://www.science.org/doi/full/10.1126/sciadv.1602614, PMC5406137). ### pets-2018 — Chua, Calandra, McAllister, Levine (2018). Deep Reinforcement Learning in a Handful of Trials using Probabilistic Dynamics Models (PETS). NeurIPS 2018, pp. 4759–4770. arXiv:1805.12114

- **Novelty threat:** partial — uncertainty-aware MPC with ensembles is standard; escalation to a mechanistic simulator inside the planner is not.

## Synthesis for this cluster

**(a) Closure / missing-physics learning.** The right default is a universal differential equation at every hierarchy level (ude-2020): a mechanistic skeleton plus a learned residual whose norm is penalised so that the mechanistic parameters stay interpretable (aphynity-2021). The residual should carry memory (Mori–Zwanzig-motivated non-Markovian closures, gupta-lermusiaux-2021), be trained *through* the solver (a posteriori) with structure-preserving constraints (sanderse-closure-review-2024, kochkov-2021), and be rejected if it is only accurate offline (learned-les-closures-2019). Where the mechanistic module is not differentiable, RL-through-the-simulator is the fallback (kurz-beck-rl-closure-2023). Symbolic distillation (sindy-2016, pysr-2023) is a downstream hypothesis generator, not a primary learner. PINN residual losses (pinn-2019) are not a default: they are ill-conditioned on the stiff, transport-dominated problems biology produces (krishnapriyan-2021) and presuppose correct equations.

**(b) Hidden-state inference.** The default should be amortised neural posterior estimation over the mechanistic simulator (npe-apt, goncalves-2020, sbi-toolbox) with learned summary networks and mandatory simulation-based-calibration and misspecification checks (schmitt-misspec); a misspecification alarm is itself evidence of missing physics and should feed (a). Latent ODE/RSSM encoders (neural-ode-2018, planet-dreamer) handle irregular sampling, but the latents are only trustworthy for intervention reasoning if trained on intervention-labelled simulator trajectories, which is the identifiability condition the theory actually gives (citris-2022, peters-causal-dynamical-2022). Constrained point estimation (sbinn-2020) is a baseline only.

**(c) Uncertainty that can drive routing.** Use heteroscedastic deep ensembles as the epistemic signal (deep-ensembles-2017, pets-2018), wrapped in split-conformal calibration on held-out mechanistic simulations so that the routing threshold has a coverage meaning (conformal-2021); use last-layer Laplace for components that must update online (laplace-redux-2021). Report calibration and sharpness explicitly (guo-calibration-2017, psaros-uq-review-2023) and track model-form error separately, since that is what fidelity allocation responds to. Do **not** use deep evidential regression as a routing trigger: its epistemic estimate is a regulariser-dependent heuristic with no second-order guarantee (evidential-regression-2020 criticisms). Do not rely on conformal coverage under shift without online simulator spot-checks, since exchangeability fails in precisely the OOD cases that matter. Differentiable-simulator gradients need a chaos diagnostic before they are trusted for training or for sensitivity-based routing (metz-2021).

**(d) Inverse design.** Pose targets at the hierarchy level where they are expressible (pezzulo-levin-2016), enumerate structurally reachable attractors and minimal override sets on the mechanistic network tier (zanudo-albert-control, network-controllability-2011 for pruning only), then optimise realisable dosed, timed policies with uncertainty-aware MPC over the learned model with mechanistic re-simulation of high-spread trajectories (pets-2018, planet-dreamer). The routing objective should be decision-oriented value of information per unit compute (foster-boed-2019-2021, rainforth-modern-bed-2024), implemented as a PonderNet-style probabilistic escalation (adaptive-computation). Baselines to beat: CellOracle, Mogrify and OT-based response models (celloracle-2023, mogrify-2016, cellot-2023).

**Prior art the programme must position against.** LED (led-2022) already alternates learned latent dynamics with bursts of the fine simulator; multi-fidelity GPs (kennedy-ohagan-2000) already allocate fidelity by uncertainty per cost; DAD already amortises sequential design. The defensible novelty is the combination: *adaptive*, uncertainty- and causally-driven fidelity routing over a hierarchy of differentiable biological simulators with interventional semantics and inverse design. Fashionable defaults to avoid: free-running neural-operator or world-model rollouts without re-anchoring (fno-2021, mp-pde-2022), evidential UQ, PINN forward solvers, and vanilla SINDy on noisy partially observed biological data.
