## Result in one paragraph

Adaptivity is possible here (oracle: fine-floor accuracy at 33 % of fine cost; 79 % of
episodes need no fine physics at all) and a learned router finds most of it (tolerance
reached at 38 % of fine cost vs 47 % for the physics-aware rule, 65 % spatial, ≥ 91 %
random), with calibrated probabilities (ECE 0.007) and near-perfect ranking (AUROC
0.993). The frontiers cross: the router wins by an order of magnitude at low budgets
through selectivity, but never reaches the numerical floor because it confidently misses
intermediate cascade members that the margin-based rule catches by refining more. The
uncertainty bonus contributes nothing, and on sustained-step interventions the router
fails with zero ensemble disagreement. All seven testable hypotheses are supported under
their pre-registered criteria; H1 and H9 only at the point estimate / under the registered
"max over one-shot and sequential" rule, and both are reported with the alternative
readings.

## What changed in our thinking

- "Learned vs heuristic" is the wrong framing when the failure condition is known: the
  learned router adds selectivity, the rule adds coverage. V1 must include a hybrid
  (physics-derived candidates, learned ranking) as a condition.
- Ensemble disagreement is not an OOD detector for this class of shift. Feature-range
  guards, conformal calibration, or explicit density estimation must be tested before any
  claim about "detecting when the surrogate is out of distribution" (H5 at V1).
- Hard tolerance-defined necessity labels are noisy for marginal cascade members and
  non-monotone episodes (19 %). A value target (expected error reduction per node) is the
  right label for the router at V1.
- The oracle is not a clean upper bound when the fine simulator's own numerical error is
  comparable to the tolerance (amp_high, multi2): it selects on noise. Reference accuracy
  must be re-validated per intervention family.

## Decision

Proceed to V1 (ADR-0005). The machinery works and failure is interpretable; the open
questions are now specific: hybrid routing, value labels, OOD detection beyond spread,
and a graded (non-fold) closure-failure condition.

## Addendum (later the same day): robustness variants

- `v0_ratio13` (ε = 0.0025, fine cost 40, ratio 13.3; 54 min): all verdicts reproduced;
  cost-to-tolerance oracle 9 %, learned 11 %, physics 18 %, adjoint 29 % of fine cost;
  learned floor again 1.4 × the numerical floor; crossing persists (learned better at
  1.5–2 × medium, physics at 3 ×). The variant's OOD table is an artefact of the
  "2 × medium" reporting budget (one fine node exceeds it) and its sigmoid family has no
  necessary nodes (folds at ±0.127); both recorded in RESULTS.md §10a.
- `v0_base0` (constant closure as the cheap default; 14 min): **adaptivity fails**. The
  coarse model's sub-threshold bias is spread over all nodes; the tolerance is unreachable
  for any subset of switchers in 78 % of episodes; oracle, learned and physics policies
  all sit at ≈ 0.05 (50 × the tolerance) and only uniform refinement reaches the floor.
  H1 is falsified in this configuration and H9 shows no differences. This is the clearest
  statement V0 produces: adaptive fidelity needs sparse, causally concentrated
  cheap-model error, and that sparsity must be measured before any adaptive claim
  (ADR-0005 amended with requirement 5).
