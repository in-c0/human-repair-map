At 50 % the one-shot router is at parity with the V0-style hard-label classifier (0.040 vs

**A caveat on the coded rule, and why it does not matter.** `verdicts.py` compares each
baseline at its validation-selected threshold and, when no threshold attains the budget, at its
cheapest point. The ensemble-uncertainty trigger cannot spend less than 0.40 of uniform fine,
so at the 30 % point it competes at 44 % more compute than the router. H1-V1 says "at fixed
compute"; the post-hoc supplement `verdicts_supplement.md` (written after the verdict was read,
same statistics, unattainable baselines listed instead of compared) gives, at 30 %, the
uncertainty-**per-cost** trigger (0.324, 0.418) against the router (0.277, 0.823): ratio 1.97,
Δ +0.405 [−0.020, +1.219], Holm p 0.38. Not ≤ 0.8, so **H1-V1 is falsified under both
readings**. At 15 % the router beats that trigger (1.46 vs 1.58, CI excluding 0) — a point at
which the hypothesis cannot pass. Reserved set (100 episodes, evaluated once at the same
thresholds): 30 % → 0.365 vs 0.360 (ratio 1.01); 50 % → 0.017 vs hard-label 0.046 (ratio 0.36,
CI includes 0). Same picture.

What the numbers say: the *spread* of the learned VoC ensemble is a better refinement signal
than its *mean prediction* at every budget the spread-triggers can reach; the mean regresses
rare, large gains toward zero (labels are zero-inflated and heavy-tailed even after the
log-compression of correction 3), whereas members disagree exactly on those rows. The written
rules are useless on this system: the medium-vs-coarse discrepancy is large for every channel
(the reduced level is far from the HH level everywhere), so the discrepancy and sensitivity
monitors cannot spend less than 54–60 % and still leave > 5 tolerances of error. This is the
reverse of V0, where the written physics rule was the strongest baseline at high budgets and
ensemble disagreement contributed nothing.

## 3. H3-V1 (selectivity vs coverage) — **not passed** (not falsified)

Precision at the router's principal-point recall (0.755): learned 0.668 vs discrepancy 0.256
(2.6×) and sensitivity × discrepancy 0.310 (2.2×) — the ≥ 1.2× clause holds. The crossing
clause fails: the router is better than the written rules at 15 % (as predicted) **and** at
50 % (not predicted: share 0.389 at 42 % vs router 0.049 at 49 %; discrepancy/sensitivity
> 5.6 at ≥ 54 %). The predicted "written rule wins by coverage at high budgets" did not happen
here; the router dominates the written rules at every budget, which is not the falsification
condition either ("dominated at every budget" or "precision not higher"). The sequential
hybrid formally passes H3 (1.84× / 1.54×, crossing true), but only because its own plateau at
0.58 makes the share rule "not worse" at 50 % — a property of the candidate filter, not of
coverage; reported, not claimed.

False-safe rate at the principal point (`verdicts.md`, table): one-shot VoC 0.17–0.24 across ID,
reserved and the four OOD families; hard-label 0.12–0.19; the uncertainty trigger 0.00 (it
refines nearly everything it is unsure about, at 40 % cost).

### 3.1 Where the router's principal-point error comes from (descriptive, post hoc)

The preregistered metric is the mean error/tolerance over the 2400 ID target rows. At the
router's 30 % point the median is 0.0 (most rows sit at the noise floor), 335 rows exceed one
tolerance, 12 exceed ten, and **one row carries 48 % of the summed error**: episode 120108
(NaT inactivation × 2.76, current clamp, spike latency), where the published HH model is silent
(latency 500 ms, the no-spike sentinel) while the level-A truth fires at 10.4 ms — a
silence-versus-firing flip worth 941 tolerances — and the router did not refine NaT (medium-only
cost 0.078). Without that row the router's mean is 0.431, at parity with the uncertainty-per-cost
trigger (0.418, whose worst row is 20). By intervention family the router's mean is 0.35–0.52
everywhere except nat_inactivation (4.15); by target, the current-clamp spike latency (5.5),
voltage RMSE (1.6) and spike count (1.0) dominate, the recovery fraction is at 0.70, the
Kf voltage-clamp targets at 0.05–0.11 and the NaT voltage-clamp targets at 0.000. The verdict
stands as preregistered; the reading is that the ensemble-spread trigger's advantage is
concentrated in rare catastrophic misses (qualitative flips of the published model), which is
exactly where a point estimate regressed toward zero fails and members disagree.

## 4. Falsification attempts (`falsification.md`; voc at its validation-selected 30 % point)

| control | err/tol | precision | recall | cost to 95 % success |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| reference (the trained router) | 0.823 | 0.67 | 0.76 | 0.347 |
| permuted labels | 6.02 | 0.07 | 0.27 | 0.87 |
| descriptors only (no simulation features) | 1.86 | 0.66 | 0.74 | 0.344 |
| simulation only (no descriptors, no rate scales) | 0.43 | 0.68 | 0.76 | 0.346 |
| target-blind | 0.86 | 0.64 | 0.74 | 0.353 |

Leave-one-family-out (held-out vs reference error on the family): density 0.47 vs 0.47, k_out
0.35 vs 0.36, kf_inactivation 0.39 vs 0.36, kf_loss 0.58 vs 0.47, kf_recovery 0.54 vs 0.52,
none 0.42 vs 0.41, temperature 0.39 vs 0.35, nat_inactivation 0.63 vs 4.15 (the reference's
catastrophic row of §3.1; the fold that did not see the family happened to refine it).

Reading: the router learns from the labels (permutation collapses it to precision 0.07); it
transfers across intervention families (no memorisation); the simulation features alone
reproduce its selection quality and the descriptors alone almost do — the two carry redundant
information about which channel an intervention touches; and **a target-blind router is nearly
as good as the target-conditioned one** (precision 0.64 vs 0.67, cost to 95 % success 0.353 vs
0.347). The protocol group (a Kf step, a Kf recovery pair, a NaT step, the current step) already
determines which channel can matter; the target identity within a group adds little here. The
"target-conditioned" part of the V1 proposition therefore reduces, on this system, to
"protocol-conditioned".

## 5. H5-V1 and H8-V1 (`verdicts_extended.md`; surrogates trained on the 320 training episodes, gates calibrated on the 80 validation episodes, evaluated on the ID test set and the four OOD families; 660 target rows per family)

**H5-V1 (compiled surrogate with calibrated distrust) — falsified.** Clause 1 holds (inference
4835× cheaper than one fine call; 157× amortised over the 800 ID queries with the 25 s of
training charged). Clause 2 fails decisively: the descriptor-only emulator is within tolerance
on **28 %** of ID rows (falsification threshold 60 %; the published HH model itself reaches
47 %, the medium + residual hybrid 62 %). Clauses 3–4 hold only through the conformal gate,
which on validation trusts the surrogate on 0.8 % of rows (coverage 0.003 on ID test) — a gate
that never trusts is trivially never falsely safe; every other gate (ensemble spread, kNN
density, range guard) declares 92–95 % of rows safe and is wrong on 92–96 % of the rows that
needed fine physics. A compiled effective model of this cell's targets, from descriptors
alone, does not exist at this training size; and no distrust gate other than "never trust"
is calibrated.

**H8-V1 (hybrid closure vs black box) — not passed.** The medium + learned-residual closure has
lower mean error than the black-box emulator on 4/4 OOD families with every paired CI
excluding 0 (block 9.6 vs 40.6, opening_step 12.7 vs 42.7, combo 31.7 vs 129.8, activation_rate
5.1 vs 27.4; within-tolerance 0.43–0.64 vs 0.25–0.34). The invalidity clause fails: the
hybrid's mechanistic discrepancy monitor detects its own out-of-tolerance rows with AUROC
0.59 (pooled OOD) against 0.79 for the emulator's ensemble spread (the hybrid's own ensemble
spread: 0.86). The structural-constraint clause also fails in the wrong direction: the hybrid
violates the medium model's constraints (non-negative spike counts, recovery fraction ≤ 1,
positive ISI and time-to-peak) on 4–7 % of rows against 1–2 % for the emulator, because an
additive residual on top of a physical value is not itself constrained. Reading: keeping the
physics in the loop halves the error and roughly doubles the within-tolerance rate of a
learned closure, but neither the closure's own invalidity monitor nor its structural
guarantees are what the hypothesis claimed; the useful invalidity signal is, again, ensemble
disagreement.

`restoration_nat_loss/summary.json`, 16 instances, 3 silent wild types skipped, 2 damaged

| search simulator | claimed restored (own simulator) | restored on fine A | on fine B (its own wild type) | on the held-out 20 pA protocol | all three | claimed but fails fine A | search cost / uniform-fine search |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| routed (medium + VoC-selected refinement) | 0.73 | **0.73** | 0.09 | 0.82 | 0.09 | 0.00 | **0.26** |
| medium only | 0.64 | 0.09 | 0.09 | 0.09 | 0.00 | **0.86** | 0.07 |
| uniform fine | 0.73 | 0.73 | 0.09 | 0.73 | 0.09 | 0.00 | 1.00 |

Applying the H10-V1 rule to this variant gives **not passed** (all-three 0.09 < 0.70; the
cost clause 0.26 ≤ 0.50 holds; not falsified: fine-A 0.73 ≥ 0.50 and no routed candidate that
claimed restoration failed fine A). Two readings, kept apart:

- *Simulator comparison (what the rung was built to test).* The routed search reproduces the
  uniform-fine search on every check — the same 8 of 11 instances restored on fine A, more on
  the held-out protocol — at 26 % of its cost, and every candidate it believes in survives the
  fine-A check. The medium-only search is confidently wrong: it declares 7 of 11 restored and
  6 of those fail on fine A (86 %); a compensation designed on the published HH model alone
  restores nothing (1/11). For functional restoration on this model, refining only where the
  router says so buys the fine simulator's reliability at a quarter of its cost; this is the
  one place in V1 where the routed simulator did what the programme's proposition says.
- *Formulation transfer (what makes it simulator-specific).* Restorations found under the
  level-A truth restore the level-B cell in 1 of 11 cases, **for every method including the
  uniform-fine search**. Two constructed fine formulations that agree on the published
  currents to 2.5–10 % disagree about what compensates a NaT loss; the compensation is a
  property of the formulation, not of the cell. Nothing about "restoration" transfers until
  the fine level is constrained by data the published model does not contain.
